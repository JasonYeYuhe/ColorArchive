# Color Psychology: Editorial Warmth

The palette leans into apricot, amber, garnet, and muted olive so the page feels human and tactile rather than sterile.

---

## Apricot Whisper Soft (#F5F1EA)
**Family:** Orange · **Lightness:** 94% · **Saturation:** 34%
**Mood:** Warmth, enthusiasm, creativity
**Associations:** Friendliness, confidence, adventure. Orange balances energy with approachability.
**Caution:** Can feel cheap or overly casual without supporting neutrals.

## Amber Silk Soft (#C9C092)
**Family:** Yellow · **Lightness:** 68% · **Saturation:** 34%
**Mood:** Optimism, clarity, warmth
**Associations:** Happiness, intellect, caution. Yellow is the most visible color and suggests openness.
**Caution:** Too much yellow can strain eyes; use sparingly as accents.

## Citrine Tone Muted (#ABAB87)
**Family:** Yellow · **Lightness:** 60% · **Saturation:** 18%
**Mood:** Optimism, clarity, warmth
**Associations:** Happiness, intellect, caution. Yellow is the most visible color and suggests openness.
**Caution:** Too much yellow can strain eyes; use sparingly as accents.

## Garnet Velvet Soft (#90475F)
**Family:** Pink · **Lightness:** 42% · **Saturation:** 34%
**Mood:** Expression, beauty, tenderness
**Associations:** Playfulness, romance, softness. Modern pinks range from bold fuchsia to quiet blush.
**Caution:** Stereotypical use feels dated; pair with unexpected neutrals.

## Olive Dusk Muted (#5C6647)
**Family:** Lime · **Lightness:** 34% · **Saturation:** 18%
**Mood:** Freshness, growth, vitality
**Associations:** Nature, renewal, youth. Lime bridges the energy of yellow with the calm of green.
**Caution:** Can feel acidic or jarring in large areas.
