## The problem

Muted text is often written as opacity instead of a colour:
`color: rgb(0 0 0 / 0.6)` for secondary copy, `rgb(255 255 255 / 0.7)` for a
label on a filled button. It's a tidy pattern. One token works on every surface,
and the text "tints" toward whatever sits behind it.

That same property is the trap. A translucent colour has no contrast ratio of its
own. The pixel people read is the blend of the text and the surface under it, so
a token like that has a different contrast on every surface you put it on. A
checker that ignores the alpha channel is really measuring solid black or solid
white, and it will say so with great confidence.

## How far off it is

The browser blends each sRGB channel as `alpha × text + (1 − alpha) × background`.
Do that by hand and measure the result against its own background with the WCAG
2 formula:

- `rgb(0 0 0 / 0.6)` on `#FFFFFF` becomes `#666666`, which measures **5.74:1**.
  Drop the alpha and the checker reports **21:1**.
- The same token on `#D4D4D8` becomes `#555556`: **5.04:1**. On `#A1A1AA` it
  becomes `#404044`: **4.03:1**, which fails AA for body text. The token didn't
  change. The card behind it did.
- `rgb(255 255 255 / 0.6)` on a near-black `#121212` measures **7.16:1**. Move it
  onto a raised `#3F3F46` surface and it drops to **4.94:1**. One step lighter,
  on `#52525B`, it's **3.99:1**. Dark themes that lighten surfaces for elevation
  hit this all the time.
- A button label at `rgb(255 255 255 / 0.7)` on `#2563EB` measures **3.34:1**
  and fails. Solid white on the same blue passes at **5.17:1**. The opacity that
  was meant to look "softer" is exactly what pushed it under the line.

The ratios above come from the WCAG 2 relative-luminance formula. They are
arithmetic, so you can check any of them yourself.

## The fix is one extra step

Contrast belongs to a **pair of opaque colours**. So work out the opaque colour
before you measure.

- **Composite first, then check.** For each surface a translucent token actually
  sits on, blend it into a solid hex. Then check that hex against that surface.
  Checking the token once, "in general", gives you a number that means nothing.
- **Find the floor, not the average.** The darkest light surface (or the lightest
  dark one) is the one that fails first. In the dark-theme example, 60% white
  passes on the base surface and fails two elevation steps up.
- **Know how low the alpha can go.** Black text needs about **0.54** alpha to
  reach 4.5:1 on white, and about **0.57** on `#D4D4D8`. White text needs about
  **0.45** on `#121212`, but **0.56** on `#3F3F46`. If your muted token is close
  to those numbers, it has no room left for a slightly darker card.
- **If a design system has more than a couple of surfaces, consider solid
  tokens.** A separate opaque `text-muted` for each surface is more tokens, but
  every one of them can be checked once and stays checked.

## Checking it on ColorArchive

The [contrast checker](https://colorarchive.org/contrast/) accepts opaque hex
only, so it can't be fooled by a stray alpha. Paste the composited colour and the
surface, and it shows the WCAG ratio next to APCA.

One honest warning about our own tool: the
[palette audit](https://colorarchive.org/palette-audit/) currently reads
`rgba()`, `hsla()` and 8-digit hex as if they were fully opaque. Paste
`rgb(0 0 0 / 0.6)` and it audits solid black. Until that changes, composite your
translucent tokens before you paste them in. Otherwise the audit will pass pairs
that would fail in the browser.
