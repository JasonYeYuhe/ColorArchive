## The problem

You build a colour ramp the way most token systems do: pick a hue, hold saturation
roughly constant, and walk lightness down in fixed steps — `50`, `100`, … `900`.
Then you write a rule into the design system: "white text on `500` and darker."

That rule holds for one hue and quietly breaks for the next one, because the `L`
in `hsl()` is not a measure of how light a colour looks. It is a geometric
coordinate, and how bright a colour actually reads depends heavily on its hue.

## How far off it is

Take the two extremes at identical HSL lightness. `hsl(60 100% 50%)` is `#FFFF00`
and `hsl(240 100% 50%)` is `#0000FF` — both sit at `L = 50%`. Their WCAG relative
luminance is **0.928** and **0.072**: a factor of about thirteen. Against a white
background the yellow measures **1.07:1** and the blue **8.59:1**.

Pure primaries are a fair target for "well, obviously." So here is a realistic
ramp step instead — `hsl(H 70% 45%)`, the kind of value a `500` or `600` token
lands on, swept across twelve hues. Contrast against white runs from **1.88:1**
at hue 60 (`#C3C322`) to **10.07:1** at hue 240 (`#2222C3`). That is a **5.4x**
spread among swatches your ramp generator calls the same step. White text clears
AA comfortably on the blue and fails outright on the yellow — and nothing in the
token name warns you.

## What to hold constant instead

OKLCH's `L` is built to track perceived lightness (Björn Ottosson's Oklab, 2020),
and it behaves far better here. Take the same twelve hues, keep HSL saturation at
70%, and this time solve each one for **OKLCH L = 0.62** rather than a fixed HSL
lightness. Contrast against white now runs **3.37:1 to 4.22:1** — a **1.25x**
spread instead of 5.4x.

The HSL lightness values that produce that even row are worth seeing, because
they show the size of the correction: **32%** for the yellow, **68%** for the
blue, with everything else in between. That gap is exactly what a fixed-`L` ramp
is ignoring.

One honest caveat: equal OKLCH lightness is **not** equal WCAG contrast. The two
models measure different things. In that same row, all twelve swatches clear the
3:1 bar for large text and UI, and **none** of them clear 4.5:1 for body text.
Perceptual uniformity buys you a ramp whose steps are comparable across hues — it
does not certify any of them.

## What to do this week

- **Stop treating the step number as a contrast guarantee.** "White on 500" is a
  claim about a measurement, so make the measurement.
- **Author ramps in OKLCH** (`oklch(62% 0.15 252)`) rather than HSL when you want
  a step to mean the same thing in every hue. It has been in all major browsers
  since 2023.
- **Measure the pairs you actually ship** — surface against body text, surface
  against muted text — not the ramp in the abstract.

Paste a block of CSS or token JSON into the [palette audit](https://colorarchive.org/palette-audit/)
and it returns every pairwise combination that fails AA, rather than one pair at a
time. To see the OKLCH coordinates for a colour you already have, the
[converter](https://colorarchive.org/convert/) shows OKLCH, OKLab, Lab and LCH
side by side.
